# Independent physical-chemistry review: Q1.2 and Q1.5

All fourteen complete Markdown answers (seven groups, two questions) were read. This review distinguishes database-supported facts, accepted domain knowledge, plausible but unverified explanation, and contradicted claims. A correct experimental table is credited independently of an incorrect explanatory sentence; a detailed explanation is not itself evidence of correctness.

## Cross-cutting interpretation rules

1. A property recorded in ThermoML may be **derived from experiment**, not directly measured. Excess molar volume here is calculated from experimental densities; kinematic and dynamic viscosity are related by eta=rho*nu; isentropic compressibility can be derived from density and sound speed; activity coefficients/Gibbs energy derived from VLE are not new independent direct measurements. Retrieval of an entry does not remove this distinction.
2. Positive excess enthalpy means endothermic mixing, and positive excess volume means expansion relative to volume-additive pure components at the same T/P. The ethanol hydrogen-bond disruption explanation is reasonable physical-chemistry context, not a unique microscopic mechanism established by those two scalar measurements.
3. Density's volume-additive baseline is mass divided by additive volume, **not the mole-fraction-weighted arithmetic mean of pure densities**. Comparing a measured density with the arithmetic mean does not establish the sign of excess volume.
4. Gravity capillary timing determines kinematic viscosity, and density converts it to dynamic viscosity. Ball methods require buoyancy/geometry corrections; concentric-cylinder torque is proportional to viscosity in the appropriate steady laminar geometry. These are accepted measurement principles. ASTM's official [D445 description](https://store.astm.org/d0445-21e01.html) supports the capillary timing principle; it does not validate every claimed instrument precision or rank all methods by accuracy.
5. A small spread among a selected set of laboratories is not a calibrated uncertainty or proof that one method is more accurate. A between-method mean offset cannot be assigned to purity, end effects, sphere calibration, or surface effects without sample/calibration metadata or controlled comparisons. Such explanations can be reasonable hypotheses when explicitly conditional.
6. Ethanol is approximately Newtonian under normal laboratory conditions, but agreement among a few methods does **not prove Newtonian behavior under all shear rates and geometries**. Accuracy does not imply that a method should report a higher viscosity.
7. Universal negative viscosity deviations, miscibility gaps, azeotropic conditions, and numerical instrument-accuracy percentages require system/condition-specific evidence. They cannot be inferred from 'polar + nonpolar' alone. NIST's [ethanol/hexane/water evaluation](https://srdata.nist.gov/solubility/sol_detail.aspx?sysID=69_78) describes the room-temperature ternary miscibility gap and identifies hexane-water as the immiscible binary; it does not support a near-25 C ethanol-hexane phase-separation boundary.

## Recommended classification in claim fractions

Use `verified_original_db` strictly for a condition-matched original raw database tuple (identity, property, T/P/composition, units and value, allowing explicitly declared display rounding). Independently checked interpolation, replicate averaging, unit conversion using an assumed density, or other calculations are `valid_derivation`, not original measured-value verification. Raw counts under an explicitly stated method-code filter are `verified_database_scope_count`, kept separate from original measurement-value fractions. Use `domain_knowledge` for accepted principles and correctly qualified qualitative interpretations. Use `unverified` for plausible causal mechanisms, unsupported numerical accuracy percentages, broad-literature method claims without sources, or exhaustive coverage claims beyond the searched scope. Mark `contradicted` as a separately identified subset of unsupported/incorrect claims, not as interchangeable with unverified. A line containing both a correct method and a false causal explanation should be split or assigned a clearly documented mixed status rather than silently counted wholly correct. Bare-model numbers that happen to round from an original raw value may receive numeric-agreement credit, but do not thereby acquire source/provenance verification.
